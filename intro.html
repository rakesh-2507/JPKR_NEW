Justice P. Kodanda Ramayya

There are lives that are remembered, and there are lives that become timeless.

There are those who achieve success, and then there are the rare few whose very existence becomes a source of strength, direction, and inspiration for generations. Justice P. Kodanda Ramayya belonged to that rare and noble fraternity.

Greatness seldom announces itself with noise. It arrives quietly, wrapped in humility. It speaks through service, sacrifice, and steadfast purpose. It leaves behind not only memories, but a moral imprint that time cannot erase. While such souls walk among us, we often see only the person. It is only later, when years have gathered around their legacy, that we begin to understand their true stature. Today, as we mark his birth centenary, we do not merely remember a man—we honor a life whose radiance continues to deepen with time.

His journey is not just a biography; it is a testament to courage, discipline, and divine grace.

Born into a humble family as the son of a devoted schoolteacher, he began life without privilege or abundance. What he possessed, however, was far greater than material advantage: an unshakable devotion to learning, uncompromising integrity, and a spirit that refused to yield to circumstance.

Those qualities carried him from modest beginnings to the distinguished Bar, from the Bar to the Bench, and from the Bench into the hearts and minds of countless people.

Yet if his legal career commanded admiration, his life beyond the courtroom inspired reverence.

He was never content with professional excellence alone. He became a guardian of our civilizational heritage, a steadfast champion of Dharma, a prolific author and publisher, and a visionary builder of institutions. He revived forgotten literary treasures, strengthened organizations devoted to Sanatana Dharma, encouraged scholars, nurtured seekers, and awakened in ordinary men and women the confidence to contribute in extraordinary ways.

The true measure of a life lies not in the heights one reaches, but in the heights to which one lifts others.

Justice Kodanda Ramayya did not merely build institutions; he built people. He did not merely preserve traditions; he restored faith in them. He did not merely leave behind books and structures; he left behind conviction, purpose, and a living legacy that continues to grow and inspire.

That, perhaps, is the highest form of leadership—to create something that endures beyond one’s own lifetime, to kindle in others the same fire of dedication, and to leave the world morally richer than one found it.

As we gather to celebrate one hundred years since his birth, we are not simply honoring a distinguished judge or an accomplished scholar. We are celebrating a life of rare completeness—a life in which professional distinction, intellectual brilliance, spiritual depth, unwavering patriotism, and selfless service came together in luminous harmony.

A century has passed since he was born. Yet his ideals remain fresh. His institutions remain vibrant. His books continue to enlighten. His vision continues to guide. His example continues to elevate.

Some lives end with time.

Some lives begin with time.

Justice P. Kodanda Ramayya’s is one such life.

May his ideals continue to light our path, may the institutions he nurtured grow from strength to strength, and may each one of us resolve to carry forward the torch he lit—with courage in our hearts, humility in our conduct, wisdom in our choices, and an unwavering commitment to Dharma.

That will be the truest tribute to this extraordinary son of Bharat.

Childhood

Kodandaramayya’s father, Sriramulu, hails from the village Modepally in Guntur district (presently within Prakasham district). The family was well-respected in the village. They had fairly large share of farmland. Kodandaramayya’s father was the third of four brothers and two sisters. All four brothers were tall and handsome, with imposing personalities. Everyone said that they took after their father, whom they lost at a very young age. Their mother had a tiny frame, she was fair and beautiful. She often warned her sons that not all of them should be present at the same place and at the same time, especially not with her in public, as she was fearful of someone casting a black eye on them.

Sriramulu was forty-one years old, and his mother, Subbamma, was sixteen when Kodandaramayya was born on October 26, 1926, in a small village, Modepally, then in in the Madras presidency (currently part of Prakasham District of Andhra Pradesh).

When the elated parents set eyes on their firstborn with wonder, pride, and a sense of fulfilment, little did they know that this bright-eyed, charming little infant would bring them name and fame, and that he would touch many hearts around him.

His mother, Subbamma, was married at the age of 9, and when she turned 11, she entered her mother-in-law’s house. In those days, it was customary to address the husband’s house as the mother-in-law’s house, even if the mother-in-law was not alive. Both her mother-in-law and father-in-law passed away by the time Subbamma entered that household. The only woman present was her elder brother-in-law’s wife, who took Subbamma under her wing and became a mother figure away from her own mother. She taught her everything. Her daughter, who was 4 years younger than Subbamma, became her best friend.

She did not think much about the age difference between her and her husband. Even if it crossed her mind at some point, she accepted it like many other girls of that time, as it was a common practice. She thought, ‘My parents married me off to this man, he is my husband, and I have to live with him for the rest of my life.’ She hoped that he would cherish her, care for her and her children, give her respect, and treat her with dignity. If he did not, she would have to bear everything and would have to nurture and venerate him and the family.
